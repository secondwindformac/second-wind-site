That Mac in the drawer still has stories to give.

Somewhere at home there's a Mac that cost real money, worked beautifully, and one day simply stopped getting updates. It isn't broken — it's abandoned.

Turn it on one more time. In 30 minutes it can be your kid's first computer, your mom's window to the grandkids, or the tool of someone who could never afford one.

Every year, millions of perfectly good Macs end up as electronic waste — while millions of kids do their homework on a phone, or not at all. We exist to cross those two lines.

Give one a second life

Three steps. Zero terminal.

You never type a command. The installer asks four simple questions and does the rest by itself.

1

Create the USB stick

Use our USB creator on any computer. It downloads the official Ubuntu image and prepares everything.

2

Plug it into your Mac

Turn the Mac on holding the Option (⌥) key and pick the yellow USB drive. That's the hardest part.

3

Answer four questions

Language, keyboard, Wi-Fi, your name. Then go make coffee — your Mac comes back reborn.

⚠️ Good to know: Second Wind replaces macOS completely — it's designed for Macs that Apple left behind. Back up your files first; the installer warns you clearly before touching the disk.

Questions people actually ask

Is this macOS?

No — and we want to be very clear about that. Second Wind does not install macOS. It installs Ubuntu (a free, legal operating system) dressed and tuned to look and feel like your old Mac: the keyboard shortcuts, the search, the dock, the style. It contains no Apple software. If what you need is real macOS with iMessage, FaceTime and Apple apps, this isn't it — check OpenCore Legacy Patcher instead. If what you need is a fast, safe, familiar computer for the web, video calls and everyday work: welcome.

Do I need to create an account?

No — and you never will. Second Wind has no accounts by design: you download, you install, done. If you buy the optional Mac Experience, your license key arrives by email: paste it into the Second Wind app and that's it. Less friction for you, less data for anyone to worry about.

Is it really free?

Yes — and we're precise about it. Second Wind is free forever: the Mac look, drivers that just work (Wi-Fi, camera, sound), the app store and updates, with all the code published in plain sight, auditable by anyone. No terminal, ever. One optional extra exists: the “Mac Experience” (⌘ shortcuts everywhere and ⌘Space search) is free for the first 30 days, then a one-time US$10 — once per Mac, yours forever. If you don't buy it, your Mac keeps working and looking exactly the same; only the ⌘ shortcuts and ⌘Space go back to Linux's. No subscriptions, no accounts. Want to help more? You can donate on Ko-fi.

Will I lose macOS and my files?

Yes — Second Wind replaces macOS entirely with Ubuntu Linux. It's meant for Macs that no longer receive Apple updates. Back up your files first (the installer reminds you before touching anything).

Is this legal? Is it a hack?

It's not a hack. Second Wind installs Ubuntu, a mainstream free operating system, plus components downloaded from their official sources. Nothing from Apple is copied or redistributed. It's your hardware — you're allowed to run what you want on it.

Why not just keep using old macOS?

Without security updates, browsers and banking sites gradually stop working — and staying online gets risky. Ubuntu 24.04 receives security updates until 2029, and modern apps (Chrome, WhatsApp, Zoom) run on it today.

I'm not technical. Honestly — can I do this?

That's exactly who we built it for. If you can plug in a USB stick and hold down one key, you can do this. You never see a terminal, and every step explains itself in plain language, in English or Spanish.

Something isn't working. How do I get help?

Open the Second Wind app (in the dock) → ⋯ → Your Mac. It checks Wi-Fi, sound, camera, keyboard, Bluetooth and battery, and shows a short report about your Mac. Click Copy report for support and paste it into an email to hello@secondwindformac.com. You see everything it contains first, and nothing is sent anywhere unless you send it yourself.

Can I go back to macOS later?

Yes — macOS can be reinstalled from Apple's own recovery (Internet Recovery, ⌘⌥R at power-on) or from a macOS USB stick, on any of these models. Your files must come from your backup.
